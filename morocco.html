    The trip

    One country, a dozen different worlds.

    Morocco floored us. Across our visits we went from the souks of Marrakesh to the dunes of the Sahara, up to the blue lanes of Chefchaouen, and out to Tangier and Nador on the coast — and every stop felt like a different country. We're building this guide city by city, starting where we did: Marrakesh.

    The red city

    Marrakesh

    The first place we visited in Morocco (May 2024, and the weather was great). Marrakesh is intense in the best way — a whirl of colour, pattern and noise — and for all that intensity we actually felt really safe the whole time. Ochre ramparts, palm groves, hidden palaces and one very blue garden.

    Marrakesh · The icon01

    Majorelle Garden

    📍 Jardin Majorelle · Gueliz

    The dreamiest spot in the city — a lush botanical garden of bamboo, cacti and towering palms threaded with walls and fountains in that unmistakable electric cobalt, "Majorelle Blue." Created by the painter Jacques Majorelle and later saved and restored by Yves Saint Laurent, it's small but ravishing, and the on-site Berber Museum is worth it too. Book a timed entry ahead — the queue gets long — and go early to beat the crowds.

    Marrakesh · Most beautiful02

    Palaces & the art of zellij

    📍 Bahia Palace, Ben Youssef Madrasa & the medina

    Behind the medina's plain walls hide astonishing courtyards — the Bahia Palace and the Ben Youssef Madrasa are wrapped floor to ceiling in carved cedar, sculpted plaster and zellij, the geometric mosaic tilework Morocco is famous for. Every doorway is a photo. Go slowly, look up at the painted ceilings, and let the patterns do their thing.

    Carved cedar & plasterEvery doorway's a photo
    Marrakesh · Most fun03

    Camel ride through the Palmeraie

    📍 The Palmeraie · Marrakesh's palm groves

    Just outside the city, the Palmeraie is a huge oasis of date palms — and the classic thing to do is ride a camel through it. They dress you up in a blue Berber robe and turban (as much fun as it sounds), then you amble out among the palms. Corny? A little. Did we love it? Completely. It's an easy, gentle intro to camels before the big Sahara trek.

    Marrakesh · Best adrenaline04

    Quad biking the palm groves

    📍 The Palmeraie & the desert edge

    For a jolt of adrenaline, hop on a quad bike and tear along the dusty tracks through the palm groves and out to the rocky desert edge. It's dusty, bumpy and a total blast — buggies are an option too if you'd rather be strapped in. Wear clothes you don't mind getting filthy and bring a scarf for the dust.

    Marrakesh · Wander05

    The medina, Koutoubia & the palm groves

    📍 Koutoubia Mosque, the medina walls & the Palmeraie

    Give yourself time to just wander — the Koutoubia, the city's great 12th-century minaret, is the landmark you'll orient by, and the ochre ramparts and scalloped Almohad arches around the old medina are endlessly photogenic. Then escape the heat under the date palms of the Palmeraie. Between the two you get both sides of Marrakesh: dense, historic city and calm green oasis.

    Landmark minaretCity & oasis

    The desert

    Merzouga & the Sahara

    We did this as one big 3-day tour, driving from Marrakesh over the High Atlas, out to the great dunes of Erg Chebbi at Merzouga, and on toward Fes. Hands down the best 3-day tour we've ever taken — an incredible guide, and an experience we'll never forget: the desert itself, riding camels over the dunes, and a sky absolutely drowning in stars.

    The desert · The drive out01

    Over the Atlas to Aït Ben Haddou

    📍 High Atlas passes & the ksar of Aït Ben Haddou

    Day one is a spectacular drive: up and over the snow-dusted Tizi n'Tichka pass in the High Atlas, then down into the red pre-Sahara. The showpiece stop is Aït Ben Haddou, a honey-coloured fortified village of stacked earthen towers you'll recognise from about a hundred films. Wander up through its lanes to the top for the view back over the palm-lined river — it's magic, and a perfect first taste of the south.

    Film-set famousSnowy mountain passes
    The desert · Best canyon02

    Todra Gorge & the Tinghir oasis

    📍 Todra Gorge · Tinghir

    The tour threads through the Todra Gorge, where sheer canyon walls rise hundreds of metres straight up on either side of a little river — you feel tiny walking through it. We stopped for mint tea on a rug right by the water, then wandered up to the huge green palm oasis of Tinghir spilling along the valley beneath bare mountains. The contrast of lush palms against red rock is unreal.

    Towering canyon wallsMint tea by the river
    The desert · Prettiest stops03

    Oasis valleys & kasbah views

    📍 The Dades & Ounila valleys, en route

    The road south is one long string of postcards — earthen kasbahs perched above rivers, ribbons of green oasis, and the snow-topped Atlas hanging in the distance. The tour stops at the best of the viewpoints, and honestly some of our favourite photos came from these unplanned pull-overs. Keep your camera close; you'll want it the whole drive.

    Endless viewpointsGreen against red
    The desert · Craft stops04

    Berber carpets & desert art

    📍 Roadside cooperatives & artisan stalls

    The drive builds in a few brilliant craft stops — a Berber carpet cooperative where they roll out rug after handwoven rug in a riot of colour and pattern, and little roadside art stalls with vivid paintings of camels, kasbahs and the blue-and-gold desert. No pressure to buy, mint tea usually included, and a lovely window into local craft. If a rug does catch your eye, haggle warmly.

    Handwoven rugsHaggle warmly
    The desert · Where we stayed05

    Kasbah nights on the way

    📍 Dades Valley & the desert-edge kasbahs

    The overnight stops are part of the joy — traditional kasbah-style guesthouses with tiled courtyards, carved doors, patterned rugs and a lantern-lit pool to cool off in after a long day on the road. Dinner is usually a big shared tagine with the group, and it's where a lot of the trip's best conversations happened. Simple, warm and full of character.

    Traditional kasbahsGroup tagine dinners
    The desert · The highlight06

    Camel trek into Erg Chebbi

    📍 Merzouga · the dunes of Erg Chebbi

    This is the moment the whole tour builds to. At Merzouga you climb aboard a camel and trek in a caravan up into Erg Chebbi — the towering, wind-sculpted orange dunes of the real Sahara — as the light turns gold. It's every desert daydream at once, and it delivered completely. This is the exact tour we did, and we can't recommend it enough.

    The desert · Golden hour07

    Sunset & sunrise on the dunes

    📍 The high dunes above the desert camp

    Once you're out in the dunes, climb a high ridge for sunset and again for sunrise — the sand shifts through pink, amber and deep orange as the sun moves, and the silence is total. These were some of the most peaceful, awe-struck moments of our whole trip. Barefoot, up the nearest dune, and just sit with it.

    Climb for sunriseTotal silence
    The desert · After dark08

    Sleeping under the stars

    📍 The desert camp at Erg Chebbi

    With no city lights for miles, the Sahara night sky is staggering — thousands of stars and the faint band of the Milky Way arcing overhead. We stayed in a desert camp among the dunes, sat out with mint tea and drums, and just watched the sky. If you do one overnight in Morocco, make it this one.

    Milky Way overheadDesert camp

    The coast

    Tangier

    Our base on the northern coast (September 2024, and the weather was lovely). Tangier is where Morocco meets the Mediterranean — a whitewashed medina tumbling down to the sea, leafy gardens, and long Atlantic beaches. We used it as our launch pad for the day trip up to Chefchaouen, which isn't far at all.

    Tangier · The old town01

    The medina & the kasbah

    📍 The Kasbah & medina of Tangier

    Tangier's old town spills down the hillside to the sea in a maze of white-and-blue lanes, scalloped stone gateways and little terraced cafés with rainbow chairs and a view over the bay. Just wander — up to the Kasbah for the panorama, then down through the medina and out to the port. Grab a mint tea somewhere with a view and watch the city go by.

    Wander freelyCafé with a view
    Tangier · Green corners02

    Gardens & leafy squares

    📍 The Mendoubia Gardens & the medina squares

    For a breather from the busy lanes, Tangier has some lovely green pockets — shady gardens of parasol pines and clipped topiary, little cobbled squares with orange trees and geraniums, and quiet benches to sit a while. It's a softer, calmer side of the city, and a nice contrast to the medina bustle.

    Shady & calmOrange trees
    Tangier · Most fun03

    Camel ride & sunset on the beach

    📍 Tangier's Atlantic beach & the Caves of Hercules

    One of our favourite Tangier afternoons: a camel ride right along the beach with the waves rolling in, then out to the legendary Caves of Hercules — a sea cave whose opening, they say, is shaped like the map of Africa — finishing with mint tea, sweets and a golden sunset over the Atlantic. Easy, scenic and a lot of fun; the sunset light on the sand is unreal.

    The blue city

    Chefchaouen

    Magically blue — genuinely one of the coolest places we've ever seen. Every wall, step and doorway of Chefchaouen's old town is washed in a hundred shades of blue, tucked up in the Rif mountains. We visited as an easy day trip from Tangier (it's not far), and just walked everywhere with our jaws on the floor.

    Chefchaouen · The highlight01

    The blue medina

    📍 The old medina of Chefchaouen

    This is the reason you come. The whole old town is painted blue — cobalt, powder, turquoise, indigo — the walls, the steps, even the ground, so wandering it feels like walking through a dream. Get lost on purpose, go early for empty lanes and soft light, and just follow whichever blue alley pulls you in. We came up from Tangier on an easy round-trip transfer, which made the day trip effortless.

    Chefchaouen · Prettiest corners02

    Blue steps & hidden corners

    📍 The stepped lanes & the Rif backdrop

    The magic is in the details — plant pots in every colour against the blue, worn stone steps climbing between the houses, and sudden gaps where the green Rif mountains rise behind the rooftops. Every few metres is a new little scene. Take your time, look up as much as down, and don't rush the climb.

    Colour on blueMountain backdrop
    Chefchaouen · Best for shopping03

    Rugs, baskets & blue shopfronts

    📍 The artisan lanes of the medina

    The shopping here is a feast for the eyes — handwoven Berber rugs, pom-pom baskets, painted ceramics and wool blankets, all popping against the blue backdrop. Even if you're not buying, the displays are half the photos. Prices are gentler than Marrakesh and the vibe is calmer, so browsing is a pleasure (haggling still expected, and warmly).

    Calmer than MarrakeshHaggle warmly
    Chefchaouen · Photo heaven04

    Chase the perfect photo

    📍 The tiled fountains, doorways & flower corners

    Chefchaouen is possibly the most photogenic place we've been — tiled fountains, flower-framed doorways and that endless blue mean you genuinely can't take a bad photo. It's so photo-friendly that hiring a local photographer for an hour is a fun splurge: they know all the best corners and the light, and you come away with the whole magical town beautifully shot.

    The coast, off the map

    Nador

    We'll be honest: Nador (June 2025) is a small, workaday city on the Mediterranean, not really a tourist town — we wouldn't put it on a first-timer's Morocco list. But it hides one absolute gem: an obscure, little-known beach entirely carpeted in seashells. If you're ever in the area, that beach alone is worth the detour.

    Nador · The gem01

    The seashell beach

    📍 An off-the-map shell beach near Nador

    This was the highlight of the whole stop — a wild, empty stretch of coast where the "sand" is actually millions upon millions of tiny seashells, piled deep in every shade of pink, cream and amber. It crunches underfoot, it's endlessly photogenic, and we pretty much had it to ourselves. One of those unexpected, only-because-we-happened-to-be-here finds that end up being unforgettable.

    Millions of shellsBarely a soul
    Nador · The rest of it02

    The lagoon & the quiet coast

    📍 Mar Chica lagoon & the Mediterranean shore

    Beyond the shell beach, Nador sits on the big Mar Chica lagoon, with calm blue water, breakwaters to stroll and quiet Mediterranean beaches where the sea turns a lovely turquoise. It's low-key and local rather than a sightseeing destination — a nice, real slice of everyday Moroccan coast if you find yourself passing through, but not somewhere we'd travel far out of the way for.
