    Beijing

    The Great Wall · Tiananmen · 5 must-see spots

    The trip

    One of our favorite trips, hands down.

    We walked a lot in Beijing — and loved every minute of it. From the vastness of Tiananmen Square to the quiet lanes of the old hutongs, the city keeps unfolding. But the Great Wall was the highlight of the whole trip for us: standing on it, watching it ripple over the mountains, is something we'll never forget. Here's exactly where we went, what we'd tell you to do differently, and the tickets we booked ahead. We visited in April and the weather was perfect — mild, clear days that made all that walking a joy. We visited in April 2026.

    The list

    5 spots worth the trip

    Spot 01 · Our #1 highlight01

    The Great Wall at Mutianyu

    📍 Mutianyu Great Wall · ~1.5 hrs north of central Beijing

    This was the moment of the whole trip for us. We chose Mutianyu over Badaling and were so glad we did — it's restored, dramatic, and far less crowded, with a cable car up and a toboggan ride back down. Our biggest tip: go with a tour group that handles the drive out to the wall. It's a long way from the city and public transport is a hassle; letting a tour sort the transfer (and often lunch) made the whole day effortless. Give yourself a few hours up top — we could've stayed all day.

    Tiananmen Square & the Forbidden City

    📍 Tiananmen Square → Forbidden City · Central Beijing

    We started at the enormous Tiananmen Square and walked straight through the Gate of Heavenly Peace into the Forbidden City — hours of imperial courtyards, halls and gates. One thing that caught a lot of people out: you must reserve your Tiananmen Square entry online at least 24 hours ahead, and bring your passport. We used the registration service below to sort it (it also works for last-minute), then paired it with a Forbidden City tour so we didn't waste the visit wandering aimlessly.

    Spot 03 · Most beautiful03

    The Temple of Heaven

    📍 Temple of Heaven Park · Southeast Beijing

    The Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests stopped us in our tracks — a perfectly round, triple-eaved temple in deep blue and gold, built entirely without nails. We loved that the surrounding park is where locals actually hang out: people practicing tai chi, dancing, playing cards. We grabbed an entry ticket in advance and wandered the whole grounds.

    Spot 04 · Most atmospheric04

    The old hutongs

    📍 Hutong lanes · Old Beijing

    Some of our favorite hours were just wandering the hutongs — the narrow lantern-strung lanes where old Beijing still lives. Courtyard homes, tiny noodle spots, red lanterns overhead at dusk. We did a walking tour to get the history and the shortcuts, then kept exploring on our own.

    Spot 05 · Best in spring05

    Cherry blossoms at Jingshan Park

    📍 Jingshan Park · Behind the Forbidden City

    We timed our trip for spring, and the blossoms were everywhere. Jingshan Park, on the hill right behind the Forbidden City, wraps its painted pavilions in pink and gives you the best panorama over the palace's golden rooftops. If you come in blossom season, don't skip the climb up.
