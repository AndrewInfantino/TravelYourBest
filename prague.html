    Prague

    Old Town · Charles Bridge · Prague Castle

    The trip

    A city out of a fairytale.

    We had an incredible time in Prague. We visited in October 2026, and it's a truly beautiful city that feels like it's straight out of a fairytale. We explored the entire Old Town and the Prague Castle complex, ate our weight in goulash and chimney cakes, and walked the Charles Bridge more than once. It rained for a bit, but the sun did shine, and we were so thankful for it.

    Our pro tips and absolute musts: do not walk in high heels, because the whole city is cobblestones. Bring an umbrella or a waterproof jacket in case of rain. And be prepared for a lot of walking! For everything else, the trams are fast, frequent and easy: we bought a 24-hour pass right on board.

    Part one

    The Old Town

    Prague's Old Town (Staré Město) is the fairytale you picture: Gothic spires, pastel palaces and winding cobbled lanes. Walking around Old Town Square was perhaps the most impressive part of the whole trip.

    Must-see01

    The Astronomical Clock show

    📍 Old Town Square

    Watching the Astronomical Clock show is a must. Every hour, crowds pack into the square below the Old Town Hall as the apostles appear and the skeleton rings its bell. The clock dates back to 1410 and is the oldest one still working. Arrive a few minutes early to get a good spot, and keep an eye on your bags in the crowd.

    Shows on the hourArrive early
    Day & night02

    Old Town Square & the Týn Church

    📍 Old Town Square

    Simply walking around Old Town Square was one of our favourite things in Prague. The twin Gothic spires of the Týn Church tower over the square, and the colourful facades all around look straight out of a storybook. Come back after dark: everything is lit up and the atmosphere is magical.

    Go at night tooMost impressive spot
    Wander03

    Cobbled lanes & the Powder Tower

    📍 Old Town

    The best way to see the Old Town is to just walk and get lost. Head through the Gothic Powder Tower, one of the old city gates, and wander the cobbled lanes towards the square. Pro tip: do not walk in high heels. The whole city is cobblestones, and you'll be doing a lot of walking.

    Wear comfy shoesLots of walking
    Our top tip04

    Take a free walking tour

    📍 Old Town, Jewish Quarter & the Castle

    We definitely recommend taking a walking tour. There are a couple of free, tip-based tours: one covers the Old Town and the Jewish Quarter, and another covers the Prague Castle complex. We had a 5 out of 5 experience with our guide at Free Walking Tour Prague. It's the best way to understand the history behind everything you're looking at.

    Part two

    Charles Bridge & the Vltava

    Walking across the Charles Bridge was one of the absolute highlights of our trip, and the views along the river are some of the prettiest in Europe.

    Absolute highlight05

    Walk the Charles Bridge

    📍 Charles Bridge (Karlův most)

    The Charles Bridge has linked the Old Town to the castle side of the city since the 14th century, and it's lined with 30 statues of saints. Walking it is an absolute must. It gets very busy in the middle of the day, so go early in the morning or around sunset for a calmer crossing.

    Go early or lateStatues of saints
    Best view06

    Climb a bridge tower

    📍 Old Town Bridge Tower or Lesser Town Bridge Tower

    We definitely recommend climbing one of the towers on the Charles Bridge. From the top you get an amazing panoramic view down the length of the bridge, across the red rooftops and up to Prague Castle. It's a small fee and well worth the climb.

    Panoramic viewsWorth the climb
    Slow down07

    Along the Vltava

    📍 The riverbanks

    Take time to walk along the Vltava River. The riverbanks give you the classic postcard view of the Charles Bridge, there are swans everywhere, and we even met a very friendly nutria on the bank! It's a lovely, quieter break from the crowds of the Old Town.

    Postcard viewsSpot the swans
    Golden hour08

    Sunset over the bridges

    📍 Above the Vltava

    For sunset, head up to a viewpoint above the river and watch the bridges of the Vltava glow as the light fades. If you have extra time, the old hilltop fortress of Vyšehrad is a quieter hidden gem with great views over the river.

    Part three

    Prague Castle

    Up on the hill across the river, the Prague Castle complex is one of the largest castle complexes in the world, with St Vitus Cathedral at its heart. It was an absolute highlight.

    Book your tickets09

    The Prague Castle complex

    📍 Hradčany

    The castle complex is huge: palaces, courtyards, the Old Royal Palace with its vaulted Vladislav Hall, and views over the whole city. Don't forget to get tickets to enter the cathedral and the castle buildings. It isn't obvious that you need them, and the ticket line is long, so book ahead and skip the queue.

    Tickets neededBook ahead

    Jaw-dropping10

    St Vitus Cathedral

    📍 Inside the castle complex

    St Vitus Cathedral is the centrepiece of the castle and absolutely stunning inside: soaring Gothic arches, a silver tomb, richly painted chapels and some of the most beautiful stained-glass windows we've ever seen. Your castle ticket covers entry. For something different, you can also see the castle by night on a guided walk with city views.

    Part four

    Eating & getting around

    Two things we loved about Prague: the hearty food and how easy the city is to get around.

    Must try11

    Goulash & chimney cakes

    📍 All over the city

    For food, we found great local restaurants by simply searching Google for local Czech food and going to the highest-rated options. The goulash (ours came in a bread bowl!) and the chimney cakes (trdelník), sweet rolled pastries you can fill with ice cream, are an absolute must.

    Try the goulashChimney cakes
    Getting around12

    Trams & the 24-hour pass

    📍 Citywide

    Prague's public transport is very effective, and the trams come so frequently, we loved them. We bought the 24-hour pass directly on the tram, which made hopping between the Old Town, the river and the castle hill really easy. Remember to validate your ticket before you ride.
