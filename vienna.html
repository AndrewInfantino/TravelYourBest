    The trip

    Imperial palaces & coffee houses.

    Vienna is pure elegance. Every street in the old town is lined with grand, pastel-coloured buildings, horse-drawn carriages clip-clop past the palaces, and there's a beautiful café on every corner. We spent our time walking the Innere Stadt, from St Stephen's Cathedral and the Graben to the Hofburg, with plenty of coffee and cake along the way.

    Our pro tips: the old town is compact and very walkable, so wear comfy shoes and explore on foot. For anything further out, like Schönbrunn Palace, the U-Bahn is fast and easy. And save room for cake: a slice of Sachertorte in a classic coffee house is a must.

    Part one

    The old town

    Vienna's Innere Stadt is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, packed with Gothic and baroque landmarks.

    Must-see01

    St Stephen's Cathedral

    📍 Stephansplatz

    St Stephen's Cathedral (Stephansdom) is the heart of Vienna. Its Gothic spire towers over the old town and its roof is covered in a colourful zig-zag pattern of glazed tiles. Step inside to see the nave, and if you have the energy, climb the south tower for views over the city.

    The Graben & the Plague Column

    📍 Graben

    The Graben is one of Vienna's most elegant streets, lined with grand buildings, cafés and shops. In the middle stands the baroque Plague Column, covered in gold and white sculptures. It's always busy, and it's the perfect walk between the cathedral and the Hofburg.

    Elegant shopping streetGolden details
    Hidden square03

    Neuer Markt & the Donnerbrunnen

    📍 Neuer Markt

    Just a few steps from the busy shopping streets, Neuer Markt is a calmer square surrounded by beautiful facades. At its centre is the Donnerbrunnen, a baroque fountain with bronze figures representing Austria's rivers.

    Quieter squareBaroque fountain
    Classic Vienna04

    The Fiaker horse carriages

    📍 All around the old town

    You'll see the traditional Fiaker horse-drawn carriages all over the old town, their drivers dressed in bowler hats. A short ride is a classic (and very romantic) way to see the sights around the Ringstrasse and the Hofburg.

    Part two

    Palaces & coffee houses

    Imperial Habsburg grandeur, and the café culture Vienna is famous for.

    Imperial Vienna05

    The Hofburg

    📍 Heldenplatz & the Hofburg

    The Hofburg was the winter palace of the Habsburgs for over 600 years, and it's enormous. Walk around its courtyards and the green lawns of the Heldenplatz and Volksgarten. Inside you'll find the Imperial Apartments and the Sisi Museum, and outside the city, Schönbrunn Palace is the other must-see palace.

    Wander06

    Freyung & the Schottenkirche

    📍 Freyung

    The Freyung is a beautiful cobbled square in the old town, framed by grand baroque palaces and the yellow Schottenkirche, one of Vienna's oldest churches. It's a lovely, quieter corner to explore on your way to Café Central.

    Cobbled squareBaroque palaces
    Must try07

    Coffee houses & the Sachertorte

    📍 Café Central & Hotel Sacher

    Vienna's coffee-house culture is famous, and Café Central, inside the grand Palais Ferstel, is the most iconic of all. Expect a line, but it's worth it. Across from the State Opera, Hotel Sacher is home to the original Sachertorte, the famous chocolate cake. Both are a must.
