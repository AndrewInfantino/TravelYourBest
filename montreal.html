    The trip

    This one's personal — it's home.

    Montreal is where I (Andrew) was born and raised, so this guide comes straight from the heart. Melody first visited in 2023 and fell for it too, and we come back regularly to see family. It's a beautiful, bilingual, endlessly vibrant city — genuinely one of the best places we know to live, to raise a family and to explore. What we love most is that Montreal has four truly distinct seasons, and every one is worth showing up for. Summers are one long run of festivals and terrace nights; autumn sets the whole region on fire with red-and-gold leaves, apple orchards and pumpkin patches; and winter turns it into a playground — we've gone tubing, skiing, dog sledding and skating, all within reach of the city. And of course, you've got to love hockey season. Here's how we'd spend time in our city, across the seasons, and just beyond it.

    Area one

    Montreal

    A walkable, creative, festival-loving city where French and English mingle on every corner. Old-world charm down by the river, a buzzing modern downtown, green space everywhere, and food worth planning your whole trip around.

    By the river01

    The Old Port & La Grande Roue

    📍 Vieux-Port de Montréal

    The Old Port is where we always start. It's a gorgeous stretch of waterfront along the St. Lawrence — cobblestones, patios, the big Grande Roue observation wheel (the tallest in Canada, with a view over the whole city), and the classic "J ❤ MTL" sign for the obligatory photo. Ride the wheel at golden hour, then hop on a river cruise to see the skyline and Habitat 67 from the water.

    The lookout02

    Mount Royal & the skyline

    📍 Kondiaronk Belvedere, Mount Royal

    The city is named after the little mountain at its heart, and the Mount Royal lookout (the Kondiaronk Belvedere by the old chalet) gives you the definitive Montreal view — the whole downtown skyline spread out below, especially magic at dusk when the lights come on. The park itself, designed by the same man behind New York's Central Park, is a huge green playground for hiking, picnics and winter skating.

    The iconic city viewGo at dusk
    The heart of it03

    Old Montreal, downtown & the food

    📍 Vieux-Montréal & Rue Sainte-Catherine

    Cobblestoned Old Montreal is all European stone facades, horse carriages and Notre-Dame Basilica, while downtown — Rue Sainte-Catherine, the "Bonjour Montréal" sign and the rainbow glass of the Palais des congrès — brings the modern energy. Wander both on foot. And do not leave without eating: this is a world food city, so come hungry for poutine, a wood-fired Montreal bagel and a mountain of smoked meat. A local food tour is the tastiest way to do it.

    The landmark04

    Saint Joseph's Oratory

    📍 Oratoire Saint-Joseph, Mount Royal

    Perched on the slope of Mount Royal, the Oratoire Saint-Joseph is the largest church in Canada and its huge copper dome is a Montreal landmark you can spot from across the city. Climb the steps (or take the escalators) for sweeping views over the north side of town — it's peaceful, grand and worth the trip up the hill.

    Iconic domeCity views
    Only in MTL05

    The Gibeau Orange Julep

    📍 Gibeau Orange Julep, Décarie

    Every city has its quirky icon, and ours is a three-storey orange. The Gibeau Orange Julep is a beloved retro drive-in shaped like a giant orange, serving its secret-recipe frothy orange drink since the 1940s. It's pure Montreal nostalgia — grab a julep, snap the photo, and soak up a slice of local history.

    Retro roadside iconFun photo stop
    Green escape06

    Nature inside the city

    📍 Parc-nature du Bois-de-l'Île-Bizard

    One of the things we love most about Montreal is how much nature sits right inside it. The Île-Bizard nature park is a favourite — long wooden boardwalks stretch out over marshes and lily ponds full of turtles and birds, and it feels a world away from downtown despite being minutes from it. Bring a picnic and slow right down.

    Boardwalks & marshesEasy nature
    Summer nights07

    Crescent Street & summer in the city

    📍 Crescent Street & the West Island

    Montreal in summer is unbeatable — festival season (jazz, comedy, fireworks), buzzing terraces and warm nights out. Downtown's Crescent Street, watched over by the towering Leonard Cohen mural, is the classic spot for nightlife, while the leafy West Island (where I grew up) shows the calmer, greener, family side of the city. It's a place that knows how to make the most of the warm months.

    Festival citySummer terraces
    Hockey night08

    Habs hockey at the Bell Centre

    📍 Centre Bell, downtown Montreal

    If you're here in season, catch the Montreal Canadiens — the most storied franchise in hockey — at the electric Bell Centre. Even if you're not a huge fan, the pre-game light show and 21,000 roaring locals are an experience in themselves. Hockey is basically a religion here, and there's nothing quite like a Habs night in Montreal.

    Iconic atmosphereBook ahead in season

    Area two

    Autumn colours

    If we had to pick a season, it might be this one. From late September through October the forests around Montreal explode into red, orange and gold — and the whole city leans into it with apple orchards, pumpkin patches and crisp blue-sky days. It's pure magic, and endlessly photogenic.

    Leaf-peeping09

    The fall colours

    📍 West Island parks & Sainte-Anne-de-Bellevue

    You don't have to go far for the leaves — Montreal's own parks and the leafy West Island (where I grew up) turn absolutely spectacular. We love wandering the trails around Sainte-Anne-de-Bellevue and the West Island's riverside parks, kicking through fallen leaves under canopies of fiery maples. Bring a warm layer, a camera, and time it for golden hour — the light through red maples is unreal.

    Peak foliage in OctoberMost photogenic season
    Fall tradition10

    Apple picking

    📍 Orchards around Montreal (Rougemont, Oka & the West Island)

    Apple picking is a Quebec autumn tradition, and we do it every single year. Orchards ring the city — you grab a bag, wander the rows and pick (and taste) your way through a dozen varieties, usually with fresh cider, warm doughnuts and a big red barn in the mix. It's the coziest, most quintessentially fall day out you can have.

    Cider & doughnutsClassic autumn day
    October fun11

    Pumpkin picking

    📍 Pumpkin farms around Montreal

    Right alongside the apples come the pumpkins. The farms around Montreal open their fields in October so you can wander a sea of orange and haul home the perfect one to carve. It's ridiculously fun for photos and a lovely, low-key way to spend a crisp autumn afternoon in the run-up to Halloween.

    Sea of orangeGreat for photos

    Area three

    The Laurentians

    One of the best things about Montreal is everything around it. Head an hour or two north into the Laurentian mountains and you hit a string of pretty towns that come alive in winter — snowy villages, ski hills and frozen lakes made for adventure. This is where we come to ski, tube, dog sled and skate all winter long.

    The mountain12

    Mont Tremblant

    📍 Mont-Tremblant, ~1.5 hrs north of Montreal

    Mont Tremblant is the Laurentians' star — a storybook pedestrian village of candy-coloured buildings tumbling down to the base of the biggest ski mountain in eastern Canada. In winter it's magic: ski or snowboard the slopes by day, then wander the snow-globe village past giant decorated trees and snow-caked evergreens for hot chocolate and that cosy alpine glow. It's an easy, gorgeous day trip or weekend from the city — and lovely in summer and fall too.

    Skiing & snowboardingSnow-globe village
    Winter playground13

    Winter fun in Saint-Sauveur

    📍 Saint-Sauveur, Laurentians

    The ski town of Saint-Sauveur is our go-to for winter play. We've gone dog sledding across a frozen lake behind an excited team of huskies, and spent whole afternoons snow tubing — flying down groomed lanes on an inner tube and riding the lift back up to do it all over again. Add skating and skiing nearby and you've got a full winter's worth of fun packed into one pretty little town.

    Dog sledding & tubingFull winter playground

    Area four

    Parc Oméga

    Another brilliant day out from the city — a safari-style wildlife park where you drive slowly through the forest and Canada's animals come right up to your window. It's a hit with families and honestly just as fun for grown-ups, and it's stunning when the autumn colours are out.

    Wildlife drive14

    A wildlife safari by car

    📍 Parc Oméga, Montebello (~1 hr from Montreal)

    Parc Oméga is a drive-through wildlife park where you wind through forests, meadows and lakes past bison, elk, wolves, bears and deer roaming free. The stars are the friendly deer, who'll walk right up to your car window looking for a snack (you can buy carrots at the gate) — it's hilarious and genuinely magical, and majestic elk wander the shorelines too. Go in autumn for the blazing colours or winter for the snowy scenery.

    Animals at your windowGreat for families
