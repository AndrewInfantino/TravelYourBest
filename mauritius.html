    The trip

    A complete adventure.

    We'd describe Mauritius as a complete adventure. We visited in September 2026 and were very fortunate to have great weather. The island is bigger than you'd think, and there's so much to do: beautiful beaches, great boating, waterfalls, wild monkeys and dolphins, and a lot of hiking. We spent a full week here, and with friendly people everywhere and amazing tours to discover its natural beauty, it was an incredible experience.

    Getting around: rent a car. We rented from IRF Car Rental LTD, and they were incredible: over 4.9 stars on Google, great communication, and a very fair price of only around €160 for the full week. One note: they drive on the left here, which was a bit of a learning curve for us, but not as difficult as we'd thought!

    Our tip: because a car gives you so much flexibility, we booked a different Airbnb or Booking.com stay every night, so we woke up in a new part of the island each day and never wasted time driving back and forth.

    Part one

    Le Morne & the west coast

    This is the side of the island we fell hardest for. The west coast gets the calmest water, the softest sand and, every single evening, the sunsets.

    Our favourite01

    The beaches of Le Morne

    📍 Le Morne peninsula, south-west

    By far the best beaches in Mauritius, in our opinion. The whole area is truly beautiful: powder-white sand, crystal-clear water and the huge Le Morne Brabant mountain rising straight up behind the palm trees. It's a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the view of the mountain from the sand is one you won't forget. If you're up for it, you can also hike to the top. It's a serious climb, so go with a local guide.

    Road-trip life02

    Beach to beach along the coast

    📍 The west coast, from Le Morne northwards

    One of our favourite things about having a car was simply driving from beach to beach. Pull over, wade into water so clear you can see your feet, then move on to the next one. The west coast is also where you can go out by boat and see wild dolphins. We were lucky enough to swim alongside them, which was one of the most magical moments of the whole trip.

    Rent a carDolphins in the wild
    Golden hour03

    West-coast sunsets

    📍 Anywhere on the west coast

    Because the west coast faces the open ocean, the sun sets right over the water, and every evening felt like a show. Find a quiet stretch of sand, stand in the shallows and watch the sky turn orange and pink. These are some of our favourite photos from the entire trip.

    Part two

    Chamarel & the highlands

    Head inland from the south-west coast and the island turns into green mountains, gorges, waterfalls and coloured earth. This is where Mauritius really becomes a complete adventure.

    Only in Mauritius04

    The Seven Coloured Earth

    📍 Chamarel Geopark

    The Chamarel Seven Coloured Earth Geopark is one of the most unusual places we've ever seen: small rolling dunes of volcanic earth in shades of red, brown, purple and gold, sitting in the middle of the green hills. Walk the viewing platforms around them, and look out for the giant tortoises in the park too.

    Right next door05

    Chamarel Waterfall

    📍 Chamarel Geopark

    In the same park, a short walk away, is the Chamarel Waterfall, the tallest single-drop waterfall on the island, falling over 90 metres into a jungle-filled gorge. The viewpoint gives you the whole thing in one frame. Nearby, the Vallée des Couleurs nature park adds ziplines, trails and even more waterfalls if you want a full adventure day.

    Hiking & driving06

    Black River Gorges & the mountain roads

    📍 Black River Gorges National Park

    The drives through the south-west are half the fun: palm-lined roads, mountain peaks around every corner and viewpoints over the Black River Gorges National Park, the island's biggest stretch of native forest. We did a lot of hiking here, and keep your eyes on the trees: we spotted wild monkeys swinging through the branches along the way.

    Our favourite hike07

    The Seven Waterfalls hike

    📍 Tamarind Falls, near Vacoas

    The Seven Waterfalls (Tamarind Falls) hike was a highlight for us. The trail takes you along a dramatic gorge to clifftop ledges looking straight down the valley, and then down to natural pools where you can swim right under the falls. It's a proper adventure. Wear good shoes (water shoes are great for the pools), and going with a guide makes the route much easier to follow.

    Part three

    The wild south coast

    The south coast has no protective reef in places, so the Indian Ocean crashes straight into black volcanic cliffs. It's a completely different mood from the calm lagoons.

    Raw power08

    Natural rock bridges & crashing waves

    📍 The south coast

    Along the south coast you'll find natural rock bridges and cliffs carved by the ocean, with huge waves exploding against the lava rock. It's wild, loud and incredibly photogenic. Please be careful: the rocks are uneven and the waves are powerful, so stay well back from the edge and never turn your back on the sea.

    Dramatic photosStay back from the edge
    Something different09

    The World of Seashells

    📍 South of the island

    For something a little different, the World of Seashells museum has an amazing collection of shells from Mauritius and all over the world, beautifully displayed. You can get free entry when you book a quad-bike ride nearby, which makes for a fun half day of adventure plus a bit of culture.

    Part four

    The east coast islands

    Our favourite day of the whole week. From Trou d'Eau Douce, a speedboat takes you across a turquoise lagoon to little islands, sandbanks and a waterfall you can only reach by water.

    Best day of the trip10

    Island-hopping to Île aux Cerfs

    📍 Trou d'Eau Douce & Île aux Cerfs

    This was our best recommended tour. We took a boat tour to Île aux Cerfs and the nearby islands: stops at white sandbanks in the middle of the lagoon, snorkelling in unbelievably clear water, and plenty of time on the beaches of Île aux Cerfs itself. The colour of the water out here is something else.

    By boat only11

    The GRSE waterfall

    📍 Grand River South East

    Part of the same tour, the boat heads up the Grand River South East to a waterfall you can only really see from the water. The captain takes you right up to the spray. It's loud, misty and so much fun.

    You will get wetPart of the island tour
    Instagram heaven12

    Island ruins & mountain views

    📍 The islands off the east coast

    One of the small islands we stopped at has old stone ruins with windows that perfectly frame the mountains and the sea, one of the most photogenic spots of the trip. Walk around the island for views of the jagged green peaks of the east coast across the water, and bring a colourful sarong for the photos!

    Best photo spotBring a sarong

    Part five

    Port Louis & the north

    The capital and the north of the island are an easy drive from each other and make a great day out, full of colour, culture and history.

    City centre13

    Port Louis

    📍 Caudan Waterfront & the Central Market

    The capital, Port Louis, is lively and colourful. Walk under the famous floating umbrellas at the Caudan Waterfront, then wander through the market for woven baskets, spices and souvenirs. It's also the best place on the island to try local street food: think dholl puri, samosas and fresh fruit.

    A must-see14

    The Botanical Garden

    📍 Pamplemousses

    The Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam Botanical Garden in Pamplemousses is one of the oldest in the southern hemisphere, and the giant water lilies, big enough to look like they could hold a child, are the star of the show. Go in the morning when it's cooler.

    Giant lily padsGo in the morning
    Postcard views15

    Cap Malheureux & the north coast

    📍 Cap Malheureux & Grand Baie

    At the very northern tip of the island, the little red-roofed church of Notre-Dame Auxiliatrice at Cap Malheureux sits right beside the turquoise sea, with the offshore islands on the horizon. From there, the northern coast has calm bays full of fishing boats and easy beaches to explore.
